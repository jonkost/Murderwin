## 2 · Sound, music and lights: the decision

Jon asked for a clean structure for sound and lights that a person building a silly family game can run. Here it is. Nothing below is tied to a specific cue's content, so all of it is safe.

### 2a · What is already built (do not rebuild)

- **The brain** (Jon's M3 MacBook Air, wired, sleep off) runs the cue relay (`cues/`). It watches the night and does two dumb things: plays sound files from `media/` through **the Mac's own sound output**, and turns the Hue bulb outside the bedroom **red** at the start of Act 1 and **green** when the third drawing is named in Act 6. It prints counts only, never a name.
- **The sound output is an AirPlay group**: on the brain, Control Center, Sound, tick every HomePod. Every cue and every W-O-O-F News recording goes to every HomePod at once, in sync. Bulletin 2 lands in the Orangerie because the Orangerie HomePod is in the group.
- **Every cue also shows on a screen** (the bell in the corner, the words as captions). A dead speaker costs the room the sound, never the moment.
- **The study walls** (Nanoleaf Elements and Lines) are run by the study station on the M1 Air (`station/`). Built, never yet run on the real walls.

### 2b · Decided today: one source, the brain, for all sound

1. **Music comes from the brain too.** Apple Music on the brain plays a playlist into the same AirPlay group. One source means cues and music never fight over a HomePod, and nothing needs to be switched on the night.
2. **Three playlists**, named exactly:
   - `Murderwin · Arrival` : Acts 1 and 5. Period jazz and big band, upbeat, at party level.
   - `Murderwin · Investigation` : Acts 2, 4 and 6. Lower, moodier, at background level.
   - `Murderwin · Supper` : Act 3. Dinner jazz at conversation level (Jon's words, 10 September). If Jon produces a radio bed with dropouts, it goes here.
   Act 7 has no playlist: the reveal and the credits carry their own sound.
3. **The relay runs the music** (a Claude Code job, small): when the act changes it tells Music to play that act's playlist on shuffle and repeat; whenever a cue or a recording plays it **ducks** Music to a whisper and brings it back after (bulletin 2 ducks under the sounder rather than cutting, as Jon asked on 10 September); at the blackout it **stops** Music; at the start of the Relay Drawing it **stops** Music until the door opens; at `sting-reveal` it stops Music for the night. It does this with the Mac's own scripting (`osascript`, "tell application Music"), which needs one permission click on the hardware evening.
4. **Jon's manual control, until buttons exist:** the Music app on the brain. A Music row on the Host tab (play, pause, next playlist) is a nice-to-have Claude Code job, built only if time allows.
5. **Levels** are set once, on the hardware evening: each HomePod's own volume in the Home app, the brain's output volume, Music's volume for background. Play the blackout and the stings at party volume with the animals home and listen for who hides.
6. **A tenth cue sound, `chime-phone`**: one short chime for every "look at your phone" moment: the call to supper, the start of Act 4, each Act 4 round change, the call to the Parlour before Act 5, the start of Act 6. Today those moments have no sound. Jon records it with the other nine.
7. **Video for bulletin 1 and the closing bulletin** plays on the Parlour TV (Jon's MacBook Pro), with the TV's own sound. Everyone is in the Parlour for both, so the HomePods are not needed for them. Bulletin 2 stays audio on the HomePods. The relay does not play a recording that the TV is playing as video (Claude Code keeps the two from doubling up).

### Act 2 · Lights Out (about 5:30 PM, solo, about 30 minutes). Settled today, Jon's final plan.

Act 2 is short and light. It is mostly the party: people mingle, chat and help get supper ready. Jon and Susan host through it. **The game never pushes anyone.**

1. **Power returns.** The `power` sound. Every phone shows its role card on a screen that looks exactly the same for every role, held fifteen seconds. Everyone taps to continue.
2. **W-O-O-F News bulletin 1**, a video on the Parlour TV with Jon on camera as Ava Waves. It introduces what has happened at the manor and **crosses off exactly one of the five motive families** (always clarity level 3). The words also show on the Orangerie iPad. Sign-off: "Don't touch that dial! Stay tuned for more updates."
3. **Bag peek.** The phone says: take a quiet look inside your own bag, right here in the Parlour, and don't show anyone yet.
4. **The cat rotation.** The phone sends every guest to **all four cats**, one guest at a cat at a time: Granolia in the Study (upstairs), Salem in the Orangerie, Zimothy in the Library, Boo in the Entryway. The guest scans the room's code and the cat speaks **in silent text on the phone**; nothing can be overheard.
   - **Every cat gives real information: one motive hint and one bag clue about the Murderer**, both at the rung of that cat's ladder set by its affection for the guest **at the moment of the scan** (Parlour Games can move it during the act). Each cat stays in character: Salem mostly backwards, Zimothy's cheap version if he doesn't like you, Boo only speaks at 4 or above, Granolia truthful but forgetful.
   - A bag clue is **a compass, not a pointer**: it names a sense and a property, never an item and never a person, and the property is in two or three bags. Even at top affection it is only a nudge.
   - Both lines are **saved on the phone's Clues tab**. This is a clue log, never a list of what is in any bag.
   - **"Not yet, call me later."** When the phone calls a guest to a cat, every guest has this button. It moves them back in the queue, no penalty. Visits are a queue, not a schedule, so nothing breaks if someone is in the kitchen, the bathroom, on a call, or needs a moment. Every phone has the same button, so a host using it never stands out.
   - As built, and kept: a guest who has not scanned in after four minutes, or is still with a cat after five, is moved on. The Host tab counts "Cat visits: N of M guests have seen all four".
5. **The Parlour, between visits.** Usually four to six people are waiting there. **Sir Irwin's sharing prompts run on the Parlour TV** (section 9.3) and change every three minutes: "Show someone something from your bag that makes a sound." The guest chooses what to share, so the Murderer can share something harmless. **Nothing from the Parlour is recorded.** Players link what they saw in the Parlour with the cat clues on their phones.
6. **Five Parlour Games** pop up on every phone at random times during the act, never back to back.
7. **The chime to supper** (`chime-phone`) waits until everyone has seen all four cats, **or Jon presses Supper's ready on the Host tab**. A guest with cats still unvisited then gets those cats' lines on the phone at check-in (as built: "they still get their real hint"). The phones say "That's the chime! Supper is served in the Orangerie."

### Act 4 · Behind Closed Doors (about an hour, pairs and trios). Settled today, Jon's plan.

**How it starts**
1. When everyone has settled after supper, Jon presses **Start Act 4** on the Host tab. Every phone gets a two-minute warning.
2. `chime-phone`, and a silent title card with jazz on the Parlour TV: "Act 4 · Behind Closed Doors. Look at your phone and find your group."
3. Each phone shows its group as character portraits. Everyone taps **Locked in**. (Another way people learn who is which character.) There is never a "waiting for other players" screen: while a group waits, the phone shows the group card and Sir Irwin wagging.
4. `chime-phone` sends the groups to their first rooms.

**The groups:** three to five groups, in pairs and trios. The Murderer and the Ghost are never in the same group. Each group visits **all four game rooms**. Groups not in a game room wait in the Parlour. Information is shared only inside your group.

**The four game rooms** (each has a printed code the group scans on arrival, the same codes as Act 2):

| Room | Host | Game | Reward |

## 4 · The rules of the game, current

- **Rule 1: the host plays.** Jon is host, builder and player. He never learns an answer. Susan plays too. Jenna is the Steward and never plays.

### 9.6 · Salem's Motion Game (the Orangerie, Act 4)

**How it works**
- The group scans Salem's code. Their phones join the game. Everyone watches the string lights; the phones only sense movement (each phone also tints its whole screen the same colour, so the game works even if the lights fail, with no switch to flip).
- The server schedules every colour change with a timestamp, so all phones and the lights flip together.
- **Red means go: shake your phone. Green means freeze.** Backwards on purpose.
- A phone that moves during green, or stays still through red, is a slip. Any slip restarts the round for the whole group. Thirty seconds clean passes the round. Three rounds; the lights switch faster each round. Clearing all three unlocks Salem's reward. The ten-minute cap pays the reward anyway.
- A phone with no motion sensor says "This phone can't feel a shake. Watch the lights and cheer," and can never cause a slip.
- The string lights are mirrored by the brain from the game's public colour. If live control fails testing, the lights are decor; the phones carry the colour.

**Salem's lines** (phones)
- Intro: "Forty years I've kept these grounds, and now you want to play on 'em. Fine. Watch the lights, not your phones. RED means GO: shake that thing like it owes you money. GREEN means FREEZE. Yes, backwards. I've done everything backwards for forty years and nobody's complained."
- Ready: "Everybody got a phone? Lights on? Then quit standing on my lawn and get ready."
- Round 1: "Round one. Thirty seconds clean, no slips. Go on." Round 2: "Round two. Faster now. Don't blink." Round 3: "Round three. Fast as a sprinkler on a Sunday. Last one."
- A slip on green: "Somebody moved on GREEN. Everybody back to the start. Told you it was backwards." A slip on red: "Somebody froze on RED. That's not how we do it here. Again."
- Round passed: "Not bad. For people. Next round."
- All three cleared: "Huh. You lot can follow instructions. Backwards ones, anyway. Here's what I know." then the Act 4 bag hint.
- Cap: "Time's up. The sprinklers are on a timer. Here's what I know anyway, since you tried." then the bag hint.

### 15.3 · Packing rules

Ten bags, 4 or 5 items each. Claude tested this stock on 24 October, and it packed all ten bags in 60 of 60 tries.

What changed:

1. **One food per bag, as a pair.** Two single candies (Kisses, Airheads, peppermint candy, cinnamon candy, ginger chews), or one pack, tube or zip bag (Goldfish, Swedish Fish, Sour Patch Kids, M&M's Minis, ginger snaps). A pack already holds plenty. This replaces "two copies of each taste item".
2. **At least one touch item per bag.**
3. **Extras fill the bag to 4 or 5.** Anything that isn't food, including a second touch item.
4. **No more one-item-per-sense slots.** This replaces "six objects, one per sense plus a spare". Every wrapper already makes a sound, and every snack already smells.
5. **Every bag needs at least 2 clue words that sit in 2 or 3 bags. Aim for 3**, so clues still work when 2 bags sit out. In the test, with 8 of 10 bags in play, 91% of bags kept 2 clue words when packed for 2, and 99% when packed for 3. Packing for 3 is harder (33 of 60 quick tries), so fall back to 2 if it must.
6. **A word counts once per bag**, whatever its sense.

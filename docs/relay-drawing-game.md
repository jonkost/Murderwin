# Sir Irwin Needs a Hand: the Relay Drawing (Act 6, the Big Solve)

The group puzzle that ends the investigation. First written 13 October 2026. **Rewritten on
23 October 2026 to match what is built**, from `docs/handoff-2026-10-22.md` §6c and §7 and the
Claude Doc "The Relay Drawing · How to Play" (the full rules, the art samples and Irwin's
slides). Everything here is structure. It is safe for you to read: it never shows a real
picture.

**Where the settled parts come from:** the Canon page (The seven acts, Puzzles, Hardware), the
handoff and the How to Play doc above, the act skeletons (`chats/docs/sirwin-manor-act-skeletons.md`
§7, the trace ladder), the traces doc (`chats/docs/sirwin-manor-traces.md`, the look-alike

client rules in `CLAUDE.md`. Where this doc goes past those, it says so under "Decisions".

**Updated 24 October 2026** (the 23 October handoff): the game is called **Sir Irwin Needs a
Hand**, with the line *"Three drawings open the door. He only has paws."* on Irwin's first slide.
The story behind the name: Sir Irwin wants into the bedroom; he's a dog, so a closed door is
torture; the door only opens after three drawings, and he only has paws. The living room is
**the Parlour** everywhere. The bedroom screen is now Susan's work MacBook Pro, moved in after
Act 4 (the iPad Pro no longer goes there). There is no freeze date.

---

## 1. What it is, in one breath

Everyone is in the Parlour, looking at the TV. Sir Irwin explains the game on the TV. Then
the game calls one player, by their character. That person walks to the paper in the Library,
where they cannot see the TV. Only then does the TV show one square of a picture. The room
shouts how to draw it. Ten seconds. The next player is called. After a practice drawing and
three real ones, the room has named all three real pictures. The light by the bedroom turns
green, and the bedroom screen shows the murderer's trace, as clearly as the Ghost and the
Murderer fought all night to make it.

Seven people yelling contradictory directions at one person with a marker is the entire joke.

## 2. Where it sits in Act 6

Act 6 is in the Parlour, about 7:30pm, and runs straight into Act 7. The Host tab's beats,
in order:

1. **Two Parlour Games.** Every phone lights up with the night's last two sets, back to back,
   before the drawing. Boo hosts them. There are no hidden events in any act (22 Oct).
2. **The drawing.** This doc. Sir Irwin hosts it.
3. **The door opens.** The light by the bedroom turns from red to green. The bedroom screen
   shows the trace at the level the clarity dial reached.
4. **The deduction round**, back in the Parlour: each player publicly commits to one
   elimination. Settled by default: it comes after the door opens, as built. You may change it.
5. Act 7.

The dial has nothing to do with the drawing. The drawing is pure play. The dial only decides
what the bedroom shows once the door opens, so the hidden roles can never be accused of
spoiling the fun part.

## 4. How it plays, beat by beat

**Start.** On the Host tab, press *Next* at "The drawing" (or *Start the drawing* under *The
Big Solve*). It tells you nothing.

**Sir Irwin's rules.** The TV shows Irwin's seven rules slides, one after another, on the
server's clock. Each slide stays up long enough to read: about 5 seconds, plus 0.4 seconds a
word. The longest slide (42 words) stays up about 22 seconds. All seven take about two
minutes. Slide 3 ("A turn") shows the practice smiley's B1 square beside its words. Nothing is
recorded: anyone may read the slides aloud. Every phone says *Eyes on the main screen. Sir
Irwin is explaining.*

**A turn.**
1. **The call.** The game picks a random player: anyone, even the same person twice in a row.
   The TV shows only that player's character portrait and character name. No real names, no
   other words. Their phone buzzes: *You're up next. Walk to the paper.* They have twelve
   seconds to get to the Library (twenty at the start of each grid, to pull the paper down to
   the next grid).
2. **The square.** Only now does the TV show the square: a black screen, one white-outlined
   box, white line art, and the square's label, like *B2*. Never the whole picture, never the
   squares around it. The drawer's phone buzzes: *Draw. The room will tell you how.* A bar on
   every phone shows the ten seconds, read from the server's clock.
3. **The shouting.** The room describes the square. Words only, no pointing. *"Top left corner
   to the middle of the right side. No, curved. Curved the other way."*
4. **Time.** After ten seconds a chime sounds, the square goes, and the next player is called.

Squares come in a shuffled order, not row by row, so nobody sees the picture building from the
fragments. Empty squares are skipped and cost no turn.

The app never checks who is holding the marker. If someone cannot walk to the Library,
whoever is nearest takes their turn and nobody tells the app.

**The guess.** When the last square of a grid is drawn, the TV shows four names, A to D. (A
stand-in round, see §6, shows four small drawings instead.) Every phone says *Go and look at
the paper, then guess together on the main computer.* The party walks to the Library to look,
comes back, argues, and clicks one name on the main computer. There is no clock and no phone
vote.
- Right: a chime, and the next player is called to pull the paper down to the next grid.
- Wrong: *Not that one.* The name is crossed off on the TV and on every phone. Ten seconds of
  nothing, then the room picks again from what is left. The worst case is three wrong picks,
  and the last name standing is right. The TV never hands over the answer. A miss costs a line
  in the end credits, never progress. (Decided by Jon, 13 October.)

**Four grids.**

| Grid | Size | Squares drawn | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Practice | 3 × 3 | All nine | A smiley face. Public: everyone learns the game on it. |
| Round 1 | 3 × 3 | 3 to 9 | A simple object. |
| Round 2 | 4 × 4 | 4 to 11 | A slightly harder object. |
| Round 3 | 5 × 5 | 5 to 13 | A pattern: not complicated, but more sophisticated. |

Columns are letters (A, B, C, then D and E on the bigger grids), rows are numbers, and A1 is
the top-left square.

**The door.** Name the third real picture and the hall light goes green. The TV shows Irwin's
door slide (*"Pawsome! The light by the door went from dark gray to... lighter gray!..."*)
above *The door is open*, and the door sting plays. At the same moment the bedroom screen shows
the trace, with Sir Irwin's line for that level. Every phone says *The door is open. The light
outside the bedroom has turned green. Go and see.* Everyone goes to the bedroom. The trace
stays up there until Act 7. It comes back on the Parlour TV during Act 7's two minutes of
picks, so nobody has to remember it.

**What the bedroom shows** is the murderer's trace at one of five levels, set by the clarity
dial and nothing else. The ladder changed on 22 October 2026 (Jon):

| Level | What you get |
|---|---|
| 1 | The picture in black and white, hazy. |
| 2 | The picture in colour, less hazy. |
| 3 | Two similar options side by side, both traces of people at the party. One is the real one. |
| 4 | Just the picture, clear, no name. |
| 5 | The picture and the name of the trace. |

The same ladder pays the Ghost's trace in the Act 4 study (`docs/solving-the-case.md`). The
second option at level 3 is never the Ghost's trace here, and never the Murderer's in the study.

## 6. Where the pictures come from

**The three real pictures (changed 22 October).** They come from a sealed bank of simple
thick-line pictures, up to three per round, each with three wrong names for the guess. A
command you run makes them blind (`npm --prefix scripts run relay-pictures`). It prints counts
and PASS or FAIL only. On the party night the game picks one picture per round at random. You
never see the bank: you see each square when everyone else does.

**What a picture may never be.** The bank check refuses, and drops without showing, any
picture whose name or any of its three wrong names is:
- one of the style samples: the smiley, the house, the diamond pattern;
- a star (your example of the art) or a teapot (the command's old example name);
- a trace object (boots, spectacles, a candle, a cup and the rest): drawing one at the finale
  would read as a clue;
- an object on a motive card (pizza, a toilet roll, a phone, a charger, a door, a shopping cart
  and the rest), for the same reason.

The command also asks for none of these. A round with no good picture left uses a stand-in.

**The practice smiley** is public. It fills the whole 3 × 3 grid, square by square, as the How
to Play doc describes. No single square gives it away.

**Stand-ins.** Rehearsals never read the bank. They, and any round the bank has no good picture
for, use stand-in grids the server makes up on the spot from a random seed: straight lines,
arcs, circles and dots, under rules that make a shape (round 1), a shape with an extra on it
(round 2), or a pattern (round 3). Their four options are small drawings: the real one plus
three decoys made by changing it.

Rules every picture keeps (most come from the practice player):
- Each drawn square holds one or two strokes. Strokes start and end on simple points (corners,
  the middle of a side, the centre, the quarter points) so the room can say them.
- Strokes in neighbouring squares meet at the shared edge, so the picture joins up.
- No single square gives the picture away.
- The squares drawn stay inside each round's limit (§4), so the drawing fits its time.

Extra rules for the stand-ins:
- No stroke runs along a grid line, where the pre-drawn lines would hide it. No stroke is too
  small to describe.
- Round 3's pattern must not look the same turned a quarter turn, or the turned decoy would be
  right too.
- Decoys must be tellable apart: round 1 wholly different, round 2 the same shape with a
  different extra, round 3 one turned and one with a single stroke moved.
- Dots: two in round 2, three in round 3, none in round 1.

## For Claude Code (technical)

**Changed 22 and 23 Oct.** Rebuilt to handoff §7: the practice, the sealed bank, a random
drawer with repeats, the walk before the square, the guess on the living kiosk, Irwin's slides
timed by length, the door slide. What follows is the code as it stands.

**State.** `nights/{nightId}/public/relay` (public, read by phones and kiosks):
`{ state: idle|intro|walk|turn|vote|lockout|done, grid: 1..4 (1 is the practice), gridCount,
round: 'Practice'|'Round 1'..'Round 3', gridSize, gridTurns, gridDone[], turnIndex, turnsTotal,
drawerKey, drawerName, drawerCharacterId, nextKey, nextName, nextCharacterId,
cell: { label, strokes[] }, slides[], introEndsAt, walkEndsAt, turnEndsAt, lockoutEndsAt,
voteEndsAt (always null), options: [{ name } | { size, cells }] x4, crossed[], lastResult,
doorOpen, doorSlide, startedAt, finishedAt, stats, timing }`. `drawerName` and `nextName` are
character names, never a guest's own name. Only the current cell is ever in the public doc, and
the options only in `vote` and `lockout`. `nights/{nightId}/private/relay` (no client reads):
`{ seed, grids[4], order[], votes, crossed, misses[4], fromBank }`.

**Irwin's slides.** `relay.IRWIN_SLIDES` (seven) are written to `public/relay.slides` at start.
Each carries its own `ms` (`slideMs(text)` = `SLIDE_BASE_MS` 5 s + `SLIDE_WORD_MS` 0.4 s a
word) and slide 3 carries `cell`, the practice smiley's B1 (`relay-bank.PRACTICE.cells.B1`,
public). `introEndsAt` is start plus `INTRO_MS`, the sum. The kiosk's `screen.slideAt(relay, now)` picks the slide showing; a doc
with no per-slide `ms` falls back to `timing.slideMs`. `IRWIN_DOOR_SLIDE` is written as
`doorSlide` when the last round is named; `screen.doorSlideOf` puts it on the TV above "The
door is open" until the trace takes the screen.

**Generation.** `relay.start` (host command `relay-start`, or the beat's `relay:start` action)
reads `cases/{nightId}` and, only when its `rehearsal === false`, `sealed/relay_pictures`
(`relay-bank.BANK_DOC`). `relayGrids({ bank, standIn: generateSet(seed), seed })` returns four
grids: the public `PRACTICE` smiley, then for each round one random bank picture that passes
`checkPicture(p, round)`, else that round's stand-in (`fromBank` counts the rounds the bank
filled). `gridOf` shuffles the drawn squares and the four names with a seeded hash.
`turnOrder(keys, turns)` draws a random roster key per turn with `crypto.randomInt`, repeats
allowed. `start` refuses a set over `MAX_TURNS` = 49 (`DRAW_BUDGET_MS` 18 min / (10 s + 12 s));
`ROUND_CAPS` {1: 9, 2: 11, 3: 13} and the stand-ins' `CELL_CAPS` [9, 11, 12] keep every set at
42 turns or fewer with the practice. Stand-in strokes sit on a per-cell anchor lattice as
`{ kind: line|arc|circle|dot, from, to, bulge }` / `{ at, r }`, rendered to SVG on the clients;
`relay-gen` keeps the §6 stand-in rules as unit tests across many seeds.

**The bank.** `sealed/relay_pictures = { pictures: [{ id, round: 1|2|3, name, decoys[3],
cells: { A1: [stroke] } }], generatedAt }`, strokes in cell-local 0..1. `checkPicture` returns
generic reason codes and never a name or a stroke: `name`, `decoys` (exactly three, all four
names different), `disqualified` (the name or any decoy says a word in `DISQUALIFIED`: the
style samples, a star, a teapot), `never` (the name or any decoy says a word in `NEVER_WORDS` =
`TRACE_WORDS` + `MOTIVE_WORDS`; `namesAny` also matches plurals and longer words), `squares`
(between the grid size and the round's cap), `label`, `strokes` (at most two a square), `kind`,
`bounds`, `bulge`, `radius`. `scripts/generate-relay-pictures.mjs` (`npm --prefix scripts run relay-pictures`;
`--dry` counts what is missing and calls nothing) tops each round up to `PER_ROUND` = 3 good
pictures, never touches a picture already there (one that fails today's check is kept but never
used, and its round is topped up), drops and counts any new one that fails the check, prints
counts and PASS/FAIL only through `say()`, redacts errors, and exits 1 while a round is short.
No Steward op reads the bank (stewardPictures answered no, 24 Oct).

**Driving the clock.** Cloud Functions do not run timers. The callable `relayAdvance({ nightId })`
→ `run.advanceRelay` → `relay.advance` moves `intro → walk` at `introEndsAt` (the first walk
of a grid is `PULL_MS`, 20 s), `walk → turn` at `walkEndsAt` (the square is written only now),
`turn → walk` (next drawer, `WALK_MS` 12 s) or `turn → vote` when the grid's last square is
done, and `lockout → vote` at `lockoutEndsAt`. It refuses early calls, moves once per deadline,
and holds while `nights/{id}.phase === 'paused'`. The living kiosk calls it on schedule; any
phone may too (`Relay.jsx`, 2.5 s after a deadline, at most every 6 s), so the clock survives
the kiosk dying. Callers pass `mayTouchNight` (a roster phone, a kiosk with presence that night,
or an admin). Bars render from the deadlines against the client's server skew, never a local
countdown.

**The guess.** The callable `relayGuess({ nightId, pick })` → `run.guessRelay` → `relay.guess`, same gate, tapped
on the living kiosk (`Kiosk.jsx Guess`). Only in `vote`; `pick` must be 0 to 3 and not crossed.
Right → the next grid's `walk` with `PULL_MS`, or on the last grid `done` with `doorOpen: true`,
`doorSlide`, `stats: { totalSeconds, misses: [r1, r2, r3] }` (the practice is not counted) and
`public/sounds.current = { id: 'sting-door' }`. Wrong → `crossed` and `misses` updated,
`lockout` for `LOCKOUT_MS` (10 s), then `vote` again. `vote` has no clock. The `relayVote`
callable is kept only to refuse old phones ("Guess on the main computer, all together.").
`presentKeys` still fills `votesNeeded` at start; nothing waits on it.

**Guards (17 Oct, second pass).** `relay.start` returns `{ already: true, state }` when the
public state is in `RUNNING` (`intro, walk, turn, vote, lockout`) or `done`, unless `force` (then
it resets first); host command `relay-start force` is what the Host tab sends after its confirm;
`enterBeat` never forces. `relay.reset` also deletes both trace docs and sets
`public/cues.hall = 'red'`, so `deal.revealTrace`, keyed on `public/trace` existing, runs again.
`start` writes `public/relay.timing` (`relay.TIMING`: `turnMs, walkMs, pullMs, voteMs,
lockoutMs, introMs`); `Relay.jsx timingOf(relay)` reads it with the old constants as fallback.

**The door (as built, 17 Oct; updated 22 Oct).** `run.advanceRelay` wraps `relay.advance`, and
`run.guessRelay` wraps `relay.guess`; whenever the relay is `done` either one calls
`deal.revealTrace` (so a reveal that failed once is retried by the next nudge; Next into "The
door opens" and into the ballot ask again too), which reads the case and calls
`traces.workReveal`: the level from `traceLevel(dial)` (one rung per `TRACE_STEP` = 4 points,
symmetric rounding), and at level 3 a partner from `pickPartner` over `TRACE_BUCKETS` in
`functions/lib/traces.js` (in play, not the victim's, Professors last; no partner fits →
level 4). It writes, in one
batch, the whole of it to `nights/{id}/private/trace` `{ level, trace, label, partner,
placeholder, at }`, the part the room may see to `nights/{id}/public/trace` (level 3:
`{ level, images: [two ids, shuffled] }`; every other level `{ level, trace, label }`, label
only at 5; both docs carry `irwinLine` from `hosts.irwinTraceLine(level)`), plus
`public/cues { hall: 'green' }`. Idempotent. The host's Next refuses to leave "The drawing" while it runs. The
bedroom kiosk (`?kiosk=bedroom`, on Susan's MacBook Pro since 24 Oct) renders `TraceView` from the public doc
with CSS haze by level over `play/public/traces/<trace>.png`. Host command `tv-trace` sets
`public/cues.tvTrace`; with it on, the living kiosk shows the trace once the door has been open
`DOOR_HOLD_MS` (10 s) (`screen.doorStage`). A kiosk on `?kiosk=ipad` follows `livingView` with
`captionsOnly`, which shows the relay while it runs and never the trace: the iPad must be moved
to `?kiosk=bedroom`.

**Audio.** The turn chimes (start, end, right, wrong) play from the Parlour kiosk's own
speaker, not through AirPlay, because a ten-second cadence cannot absorb two seconds of
spin-up. The door sting goes through `public/sounds` (`sting-door`), named by event ID as usual.

**Stats.** `public/relay.stats: { totalSeconds, misses }` feeds the finale (`finale.js`: "Time
on the drawing", and each grid that took more than one guess).

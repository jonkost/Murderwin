# The relay drawing game (Act 6, the Big Solve)

The group puzzle that ends the investigation. Written 13 October 2026 from everything already
decided about it, with the gaps filled in. Everything here is structure: there is no picture
bank to spoil because there is no picture bank. Safe for you to read.

**Where the settled parts come from:** the Canon page (The seven acts, Puzzles, Hardware, the
open questions), the act skeletons (`chats/docs/sirwin-manor-act-skeletons.md` §7, the relay
drawing and the trace ladder), the traces doc (`chats/docs/sirwin-manor-traces.md`, the ladder
and collision buckets), the paper-roll chat from 10 September (the practice player and its
checks), and the client rules in `CLAUDE.md`. Where this doc goes past those, it says so under
"Decisions this doc makes".

---

## 1. What it is, in one breath

Everyone is in the living room looking at the TV. The TV shows one square of a grid and one
line inside it. One person is in the library at the paper roll, where they cannot see the TV.
The room shouts at them how to draw the line. Ten seconds. Black. Next person. Thirty-odd
turns later there are three drawings on the paper and the room has to say what they are. Name
all three and the red light outside the bedroom turns green. In the bedroom, the screen shows
the murderer's trace, as clearly as the Ghost and the Murderer fought all night to make it.

Seven people yelling contradictory directions at one person with a marker is the entire joke.

---

## 2. Where it sits in Act 6

Act 6 is played in the living room, about 7:30pm, and runs straight into Act 7.

1. **The merged event.** The Ghost's and the Murderer's last push lands on every phone at once.
   Must be in before the bedroom, or the last choice means nothing.
2. **The relay drawing.** This doc.
3. **The light outside the bedroom turns from red to green.**
4. **The bedroom screen shows the trace** at the level the clarity dial reached.
5. **The deduction round**, back in the living room: each player publicly commits to one
   elimination. Proposed order; see §10.
6. Act 7.

The dial has nothing to do with the drawing. The drawing is pure play. The dial only governs
what the bedroom shows once the door opens, so the hidden roles can never be accused of spoiling
the fun part.

## 3. The rooms

| Thing | Where | Job |
|---|---|---|

| The paper roll | Library wall, in the blind spot: you cannot see the TV from the marker | Three grids, pre-drawn blank on the day. |

The library is in earshot of the living room. The room can hear the drawer swear; the drawer
can hear the room disagree with itself.

## 4. How it plays, beat by beat

**Start.** The Host tab has one button: *Start the drawing.* You press it like any other act
button; it tells you nothing. The TV shows the rules in three lines and the first drawer's name.
Every phone shows the same three lines. The first drawer walks to the paper roll and pulls the
paper down until the small grid is showing.

**A turn, ten seconds.**
1. The TV shows a white square with one line in it and the cell's name: *B2.* Nothing else.
   No grid around it, no neighbours, no hint of the whole.
2. The drawer's phone buzzes: *Draw. The room will tell you how.* A bar on their phone shows
   the ten seconds running, read from the server's clock.
3. The room shouts. *"Top left corner to the middle of the right side. No, curved. Curved the
   other way."* The drawer draws something.
4. Ten seconds: the TV goes black and a chime sounds. Marker down.
5. The TV shows the next drawer's name for twelve seconds while they walk. Their phone buzzes.
   Then the next cell.

Cells come in a shuffled order, not row by row, so the room cannot see the picture building
in their heads from the fragments. Some cells hold a single dot. A ten-second turn to draw a
dot is a gift and a laugh, and the dots are there to stop anyone guessing early.

**Everyone draws.** The order is random and everyone draws at least once. With nine people and
about thirty turns, that is three or four turns each. The app never checks who is holding the
marker. If someone cannot walk to the library, whoever is nearest takes their turn and nobody
tells the app.

**The guess.** When the last cell of a grid is done, the TV says so and the whole party walks
to the library to look at the paper. Then every phone lights at once with four pictures, A to
D, and no grid lines on any of them. Everyone picks one. Majority wins; a tie goes to a random
one of the tied. The window is forty-five seconds and closes early when everyone has picked.
- Right: the TV celebrates, the next drawer pulls the paper down to the next grid.
- Wrong: that option is crossed out on every phone and the TV, ten seconds of nothing, then
  everyone picks again from what is left. This repeats until the room picks the right one.
  With four options the worst case is three wrong picks, and the last one standing is right.
  The TV never hands over the answer. Every miss costs a line on the stats reel, never
  progress. (Decided by Jon, 13 October; replaces the act skeleton's "if not, it gives you
  the answer".)

**Three grids.**

| Grid | Cells | What it draws | The four options |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 × 3, all nine drawn | A shape. Simple line work. One thing. | Four clearly different shapes. An early win. |
| 2 | 4 × 4, about eleven drawn | A shape with something abstract on it: a crescent with stars, a box with dots. | Two options share the shape and differ in the extra; you can tell on inspection. |
| 3 | 5 × 5, about thirteen drawn | Abstract. A pattern, a rosette, a lattice. No obvious name. | Two look near-identical; one of them is the real figure turned a quarter turn. |

Undrawn cells stay empty. Dot cells are drawn.

**The door.** Name the third drawing and the hall light goes green. The
TV says *The door is open.* Everyone goes to the bedroom. The screen there is already showing
the trace. It stays up until Act 7. The same picture comes back on the living room TV during
Act 7's two minutes of deliberation, so nobody has to remember it.

**What the bedroom shows** is the murderer's trace at one of five levels, set by the clarity
dial and nothing else. The ladder, the collision buckets and the engine rules are in the traces
doc and the act skeletons; this doc does not change them.

| Level | What you get |
|---|---|
| 1 | Heavy haze. Something is there. |
| 2 | Two real traces overlaid, both from people at the party. A coin flip. |
| 3 | One clear image, no label. Is that pale smear flour, chalk dust or face powder? |
| 4 | Image plus its name. |
| 5 | Name plus a line of context. |

## 5. The numbers

| | |
|---|---|
| Turn | 10 s, fixed, whatever the grid. |
| Walk between turns | 12 s. Tune in rehearsal; your dad sets it. |
| Turns in total | 9 + about 11 + about 13 = about 33. |
| Drawing time | About 12 minutes. |
| Guessing | Up to about 2 minutes a grid including the walk to look. |
| Whole game | About 18 minutes, 22 with two misses, 26 in the worst case of three wrong picks on every grid. |
| Vote window | 45 s, closes early when everyone is in. |
| Lockout after a wrong guess | 10 s. |

Act 6 is about thirty minutes. The merged event takes three, the bedroom five, the deduction
round three. It fits, with no slack. If a rehearsal runs long, the first cut is grid 2's drawn
cells (eleven down to nine), the second is the walk gap.

## 6. Where the pictures come from

**Nobody draws them.** Not you, not Susan, not Jenna. The server composes each grid on the spot
from a random seed when you press Start: straight lines, arcs, circles and dots, placed on a
small set of anchor points in each cell, under rules that make the result a shape, a shape with
an extra, or a pattern. The four options are composed by the same code: the real figure, plus
three decoys made by changing it under the rules for that grid.

This is the only way you get to play this one. You would know a drawing from its first stroke.
Random composition means there is no bank for you to have seen, no bank for Jenna to review,
and a different set every night.

Rules the generator has to keep (most of them come from the practice player):
- Every drawn cell holds one or two strokes. No stroke runs along a grid line, where the
  pre-drawn lines would hide it. No stroke is too small to describe.
- Grid 1's shape vocabulary is closed shapes and simple objects that are not on the trace list:
  no spectacles, boots, bottles, candles, cups, flowers, seeds. Drawing a boot at the finale
  would read as a clue.
- Grid 3's pattern must not look the same turned a quarter turn, or the rotated decoy would be
  the right answer too.
- Decoys must be tellable apart at the grid's intended difficulty: grid 1 wholly different,
  grid 2 one shared shape with a different extra, grid 3 one rotated and one with a single
  stroke moved.
- Dots: two in grid 2, three in grid 3, none in grid 1.

## 7. Rule 1 check

- The grids are composed by the server from a random seed at the moment you press Start and
  are never previewed. You see each cell at the same moment as everyone else.
- The paper carries only blank grids and a birthday doodle. Blank grids carry no information,
  so Jessa can draw them with you.
- The trace, the decoy trace and the level are sealed and computed by the server. The relay
  never reads the dial and never writes to it.
- The Host tab's Start button is structural, like an act button. It cannot show you anything.
- The practice player from September used twelve hand-made drawings. It is retired. Nothing
  from it carries over except its checks.

## 8. Setting up on the day

- [ ] Draw the grids on the paper roll **in reverse order**: the 5 × 5 first, roll it back in,
      then the 4 × 4, then the 3 × 3. Pulling the paper down reveals bigger grids each time.
- [ ] Cells about five inches square. Letters across the top, numbers down the side, written in
      the margin. One thick marker line for the grid, a different thickness from the drawing
      marker if you have one.
- [ ] A generous blank stretch between grids, so no two are ever visible at once.
- [ ] The front of the roll carries the *Happy Birthday Irwin* doodle, which later turns out to
      have been part of the game.
- [ ] Stand at the marker and check you cannot see the TV. Stand at the TV and check you cannot
      see the paper. Check you can hear each other.
- [ ] Marker in the tray. A spare in the tray.
- [ ] The hall bulb outside the bedroom is red from Act 1. The blackout cue must bring it back
      red when the power returns.
- [ ] Bedroom kiosk awake, sleep off, pointed at the host. Bedroom door closed.
- [ ] Run one full grid with Susan before guests arrive, from the Host tab button through to
      the vote on two phones.

## 9. If something breaks

| What fails | What happens instead |
|---|---|
| The living room kiosk dies | The wandering iPad shows the same page. Open it and set it on the TV stand. |
| The hall light does not turn green | The TV announces that the door is open. The game never depends on the bulb. |
| The bedroom kiosk dies | The living room TV shows the trace after a short "the door opens" moment. Nobody goes to the bedroom. |
| A phone misses the vote | The window closes anyway and that phone's pick is random. A no-show is never a recognisable pattern. |
| Nobody can agree and the window runs out | Majority of whoever picked. A tie is random. Windows never close early except when everyone is in. |
| The server is unreachable mid-turn | Phones show what they last saw and catch up silently. The turn clock is the server's; it resumes where the server says. |

Safe to cut after the freeze: the Hue bulb, the bedroom kiosk (use the TV). Never cut: the
reveal order, and the rule that the trace shows at the dial's level.

## 10. Decisions this doc makes

Each of these fills a gap. Say the word and any of them changes.

1. **The guess is a vote on every phone**, four pictures, majority wins. The room still argues
   out loud; the phones just take the count. Nobody has to walk to the TV and tap, and you have
   no hands free in Act 6 anyway. It reuses the Act 7 ballot window.
2. **Options are pictures, not words.** Typed answers would reject "happy face" when the answer
   was "smiley". The act skeleton already says multiple choice; this says the choices are drawn
   by the same code that drew the grid.
3. **Cells come in shuffled order** and the TV only ever shows one. Both were proposed in
   September and confirmed by the act skeleton's "the room can see the fragment, the drawer
   cannot".
4. **A twelve-second walk gap** between turns, fixed, so there is no "ready?" screen anywhere.
5. **Undrawn cells are skipped, dot cells are drawn.** The act skeleton's "dots in empty cells"
   means some empty cells get a dot; the rest stay empty and cost no turn. That is what gets the
   game to about eighteen minutes.
6. **The bedroom screen shows the trace as soon as the light goes green** and keeps it up. It
   comes back on the living room TV in Act 7.
7. **The deduction round goes after the bedroom.** The Canon lists this as open. The trace is
   the last new fact of the night, so eliminating before it would be eliminating with one eye
   shut. Back in the living room, each phone offers *Strike one: a name, or a motive.* The
   strikes appear on the TV under each player's character as they commit. Nothing is scored,
   nothing is hidden, and it hands the room its first argument of Act 7 ready-made.
8. **Everyone walks to the paper before each vote.** The paper is the puzzle; the TV never shows
   the whole drawing.

## 12. What is built (13 October 2026)

Built and tested against the fake Firestore; the phone screens checked in the browser.

- **The generator**, `functions/lib/relay-gen.js`: three grids and four options each from a
  seed, with the checks in §6 as tests across hundreds of seeds. Grid 1 draws nine cells,
  grid 2 about twelve, grid 3 about fourteen.
- **The clock**, `functions/lib/relay.js`: start, advance, vote, reset. Nineteen tests cover
  the whole run, the early close, absent phones, the lockout and re-vote until right.
- **The phones**: the drawer's prompt, the room's view of the cell, the four-picture vote, the
  lockout, and "the door is open". Preview without a night at
  `/play/?preview=relay-turn`, `relay-draw`, `relay-vote`, `relay-lockout`, `relay-done`.
- **The room screens**: `/play/?kiosk=living` on the main-room MacBook (it is also the
  clock), `/play/?kiosk=bedroom` on the bedroom MacBook (closed until the door opens; the
  trace lands there once the case generator exists).

**To run it, once deployed** (`npm run deploy`, which you run): open the living room page on
the main-room MacBook and the bedroom page on the bedroom MacBook, full screen. In Act 6,
Host tab → *Next* at "The drawing" (or *Start the drawing*). Everything else runs itself.

## For Claude Code (technical)

**State.** `nights/{nightId}/relay` (public, read by phones and kiosks):
`{ state: idle|turn|walk|vote|lockout|done, grid: 1|2|3, turnIndex, drawerUid, nextUid,
cell: { label, strokes[] }, turnEndsAt, voteEndsAt, options: [svg x4], crossed[], results{},
startedAt, finishedAt, misses, lightGreenAt }`. Only the current cell is ever in the public doc.
The full grids, the answer indices and the turn order live in `nights/{nightId}/private/relay`
(no client reads). Votes go through the callable `relayVote` and live in the private doc's
`votes` map, keyed by guest key; the public doc carries only `votesIn`.

**Driving the clock.** Cloud Functions do not run timers, so a callable `relayAdvance({ nightId,
expectedTurn })` moves the state machine when server time has passed `turnEndsAt` or
`voteEndsAt`, or when every roster phone has voted. The living room kiosk calls it on schedule;
any phone may also call it; it is idempotent on `expectedTurn` and refuses early calls. The
phone's ten-second bar is rendered from `turnEndsAt` against the phone's server skew, never from
a local countdown.

**Generation.** `relay.start` (via `hostCommand('relay-start')`) seeds a PRNG, composes the three
grids and their option sets, writes the private doc, shuffles the cell order per grid, builds a
turn order that covers every roster UID at least once before repeating anyone, and writes the
first turn. Strokes are described on a per-cell anchor lattice (corners, edge midpoints, centre,
plus quarter points) as `{ kind: line|arc|circle|dot, from, to, bulge }`, rendered to SVG on the
clients. Vocabularies: grid 1 closed shapes composed across a 2 × 2 or 3 × 3 cell span (no trace
look-alikes; the exclusion list is in code); grid 2 a grid-1 shape plus an abstract overlay
(dots, rays, stripes, a ring); grid 3 a pattern with mirror symmetry built from arcs and lines
across the 5 × 5, rejected and regenerated if its stroke set equals itself rotated 90°. Decoys:
grid 1 three other shapes; grid 2 one same-shape-different-overlay, two others; grid 3 the
rotated copy, one with a single stroke moved, one other. Generator checks are unit tests: one
or two strokes per drawn cell, no stroke collinear with a cell edge, minimum stroke length,
decoy distance thresholds per grid, dot counts, rotation check, trace exclusion.

**Votes.** Majority of votes cast; ties broken by the PRNG; absent phones get a random pick
written by the function at window close. Wrong → `crossed` updated, `state: lockout` for 10 s,
then a new vote over the remaining options, repeating until the majority lands on the answer
(at most three rounds of lockout per grid). Right → the next grid or `done`. There is no
reveal state.

**The door (as built, 17 Oct).** `run.advanceRelay` wraps `relay.advance`; when it returns
`{ moved, state: 'done' }` it calls `deal.revealTrace`, which reads the case, picks the level
with `traceLevel(dial)` (one rung per `TRACE_STEP` = 4 points, symmetric rounding), picks a
level-2 partner with `pickPartner` from `TRACE_BUCKETS` (in-play, not the victim's, Professors
last), and writes `nights/{id}/public/trace` `{ level, trace, label, context, partner,
placeholder, at }` plus `public/cues { hall: 'green' }`. Idempotent: a second call is a no-op.
The bedroom kiosk renders `TraceView` from that doc with CSS haze by level over
`play/public/traces/<trace>.png`. The living kiosk shows the same during the ballot window and
runs `Finale` from `public/finale` (`deal.finaleDoc`: `schedule` offsets, `stats` strings,
`graph.points`/`graph.running` from `cases.dialLog`, which `applyPoints` appends to). Reveal
order on screen is Murderer, Motive, Victim.

**Audio.** The turn chime and the walk cue play from the living room kiosk's own speaker,
not through AirPlay, because a ten-second cadence cannot absorb two seconds of spin-up. Longer
stings (grid solved, door open) may go through the HomePods, named by event ID as usual.

**Stats.** Write `relay.stats: { totalSeconds, missesPerGrid }` for the Act 7 reel ("total
group time on the relay puzzle", and a line per grid that took more than one pick).

**Tests.** The state machine runs against the in-memory fake Firestore in `functions/test/`:
full run with nine fake phones, early vote close, absent phone random pick, lockout and
re-vote until correct, turn order covers everyone, generator checks across a thousand seeds. Throwaway night,
nothing persisted.
